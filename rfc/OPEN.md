# Open questions found during implementation

Add one entry per ambiguity, with the RFC section and the interpretation chosen.

Drafts 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5 resolved every question found while building the library, the tooling, the first executor and the two heads; `rfc/CHANGELOG.md` says where each answer went. This file holds only what is still open; an answered item leaves a one-line pointer so the numbers cited elsewhere stay stable. Only item 11 affects whether two implementations interoperate: heads that read timestamp equality differently answer the same re-PUT differently. The design questions listed in RFC §14 (last-visit cursor, withdraw, delegation rules, retention, head-to-head trust) are tracked there, not here; the content hash it also listed is answered in item 6.

1. **A.2, A.4 — orders that wait on a person for days.** A to-do executor (A.4) holds a lease for as long as the person takes to finish the task, renewing it every half lease and polling the task tool between renewals. With many orders waiting, that is a steady load on both the head and the tool, and an executor's concurrency limit caps how many orders it can hold at once. A way to release a claim while keeping the order for itself (park and resume), or a longer lease class for orders handed to a person, would fit better. **Current:** the client holds a lease per waiting order; `connectors/todoist` defaults to 50 at once.
2. **§3.1, §14 — links are not covered by a render fixture.** 0.5 makes the link rule normative (only `http:`, `https:` and `mailto:` are linked), but `RenderResult` has no member for links, so no fixture checks it. Adding one would change the render adapter's interface for every head. **Current:** each head's own tests.
3. **A.1, §12 — leases per root, tokens per instance.** 0.5 binds a lease to the producer root, so any token under the root renews it and posts its outcome. §12 lets a head bind a token to a producer instance; two instances under one root therefore share leases and must not run the same order twice on their own. Should a head that binds tokens to instances be allowed to bind leases to them too? **Current:** per root, as A.1 and A.2 say.
4. **§8 — when a card's day is fixed.** Without `about`, a card's day is the day of its `received_at` in the head's time zone, which the head sets however it chooses. §8 does not say whether that day is fixed when the card is stored or follows a later change of zone, so after the owner travels a card could stay on the day it was filed or move with the clock. Either keeps a card on one day at a time, and neither affects interoperability. **Current** (pbcp-head): fixed when the content is stored, like `delivery` (§4.2); a re-PUT that changes `received_at` places it again, and deciding the card after a change of zone does not move it.
5. **§7 — a producer reading its own card.** A producer confirms a card from the answer to its PUT (`201` or `200` with the stored card, head-owned fields included) and later through `GET /changes`. There is no `GET /cards/{id}` for producers, and the reference head answers it with `405`. Producers connecting for the first time looked for one (issue #22). A read of one card under the token's root, with the same authorisation as sync, is a candidate for 0.8. It now has a concrete customer in the reply loop of §11.1. An agent about to send a reply wants to confirm the decision on its own card just before acting. Without such a read it replays sync from a stored cursor, or keeps every decision it has seen. Still open for 0.8. **Current:** the PUT answer and sync, as documented in `@pbcp/client` and `@pbcp/mcp`.
6. **§5, §14 — a content revision or hash on the wire.** §14 asked whether a `content_hash` (or a revision number) should separate a card's identity, its content version and its decision, to make a retry easy to tell from a change and a `409` easy to diagnose. **Answer: no hash is needed.** §5's semantic equality already tells a retry from a change. An identical re-PUT is `200` and changes nothing, `emitted_at` included. A changed one replaces an open card (`200`, a new `change_seq`) or is a `409` on a decided one. Both answers carry the stored card, so a producer can see what differs. A hash on the wire would be a second definition of "the same card" that every head and producer has to keep in step with §5's: labels compare as a set, an absent list equals `[]`, `emitted_at` does not count. Two definitions can disagree; one cannot. **Current:** equality as §5 defines it, and no hash; §14 marks the question answered.
7. **§4.1, §8 — `at` without a zone.** `at` is an instant; the person's local time comes from the head's zone (§8). A producer that knows the event's own zone (a meeting in another city) cannot say so. Options: a `tz` field (IANA name) beside `at`; or leave it to the body. **Current:** the head's zone; `about` carries the producer's local day when it differs.
8. **§4.1 — an end without a start.** An offer valid *until* Sunday has an end and no meaningful start; the rule that `until` requires `at` forces such a card onto `about` alone. Allowing `until` alone was rejected to keep the pair unambiguous. **Current:** `about` the expiry day; the time in the body.
9. **§8 — recurring subjects.** A weekly meeting is many occurrences; the caldav connector mints one card per occurrence, which is correct for a day view and noisy in a thread. Whether the protocol should name recurrence (a `recurs` field, or a `thread` convention) or leave it to producers is open. **Current:** one card per occurrence, `urgency: log`, threaded by day.
10. **§3.1 — `x-at` across the rename.** Answered; see `rfc/CHANGELOG.md`, 0.8.
11. **§2, §5 — equal timestamps written differently.** §2 lets one instant be written several ways (`2026-10-30T21:45:00Z`, `…:00.000Z`, `…:00+00:00`, `…:00-00:00`, any number of fractional digits). §2 and §5 say equality is semantic, "judged on the parsed fields, never on bytes", and §5's last bullet says every other field "compares by value", but neither says whether a timestamp's value is its text or its instant. It matters for `at` and `until`, the producer-owned timestamps that count for equality; `emitted_at` does not count, and head-owned timestamps never do. Two readings. *Text*: strict and simple; every producer must write one form every time, as it already must for ids (§5). *Instant*: what "semantic" suggests; `equal` compares the instant, as `instant()` in core already does for the `until` check. That reading needs a rule for the leap second, since `instant()` maps `:60` to `:59.999` and would make `…:59:60Z` equal to `…:59:59.999Z`. It needs fractional digits compared as a decimal (trailing zeros dropped), not truncated to milliseconds. And it applies only to fields typed as timestamps (`at`, `until`, an extension's own, §3); an unknown field's string still compares as text. Under the text reading, a producer that re-sends an unchanged subject with `…:00Z` once and `…:00.000Z` the next time makes a spurious revision: the open card is replaced (`200`, a new `change_seq`, `received_at` moves), `seen_at` is kept since body and actions are unchanged, and on a decided card it is a `409`. The reference head saw re-sent calendar cards come back as changed after a producer ran twice; the timestamp form is the suspected difference, not confirmed from the producer's request log. **Recommendation for 0.9:** the instant reading; nothing in the RFC argues against it. §2's exact-string rule is about identifiers, not timestamps, and §4.1's "MUST NOT alter them" still holds, because an identical re-PUT changes nothing and the stored card keeps the form first sent. The text change is one clause in §5's last bullet: "…compares by value; a timestamp's value is the instant it names, a leap second its own instant." **Current:** core compares the text (`equal` in `packages/core/src/card.ts`); `card.test.ts` pins it.
